What this is. A feed on discrimination worldwide, in twenty subjects. The first ten are the forms the section it follows names outright: sexism, racism, ethnicism, LGBTQIA+-phobia, classism, religious discrimination, intellectualism, ableism, dwarfism discrimination and speciesism. To those it adds age and nationality, the places discrimination is actually met — work, and access to housing, health and services — and hate crime and targeted violence. The last five carry the section's own structural claims: discriminatory actors infiltrating and hijacking institutions; division used as a route to power by leaders who are not themselves discriminatory; anti-discrimination law and whether it is enforced or merely on the books; how any of this is measured; and what is set against it. Nothing here is written or summarised: headlines and snippets are the publishers' own, every row links to the original, and no model touches the text.

Speciesism is here on the same footing as the rest. The section lists it alongside the others, so the wire carries it as a subject rather than a footnote: whether animal sentience is recognised in law, which constitutions mention animals at all, and where legal personhood has been argued for a non-human. That is the section's own measure — that only a few countries have put animal protection in their charter documents, and many have yet to acknowledge sentience.

What it is not. An opinion column about a culture-war topic is not this subject, and neither is the word in its other senses — a discriminating palate, price discrimination, discriminant analysis, the discriminating power of a test. Those are refused outright. What the wire keeps is who is treated unequally, by whom, under what law, and what follows. Every subject term is gated behind the words it must appear beside, and a story no subject will claim is refused rather than filed under a name it did not earn.

Standing. Every wire is labelled by what kind of source it is, and every row carries the label. Courts, treaty bodies & equality commissions is the bodies that rule on it — OHCHR and the special procedures, the European Court of Human Rights, the Inter-American Commission, CERD, CEDAW and CRPD, national equality commissions and ombudsmen. Research & rights monitoring is the organisations that measure it: Human Rights Watch, Amnesty, Freedom House, CIVICUS. Rights & community organisations is those arguing from inside the affected communities — ILGA World, the European Roma Rights Centre, Article 19, OMCT. Press is general news across 26 languages. Standing is provenance, not a claim that a story is important.

Weight. Separately, each story is scored on what it carries: a decision — a ruling, a law enacted or repealed, a conviction, a decriminalisation, compensation awarded (2); institutional material — a treaty body finding, a special rapporteur report, an equality commission investigation, official statistics, a peer-reviewed study (2); a measured figure such as a prevalence rate or the number of countries (1); a pending decision with a date attached (1); a named jurisdiction (1); and a primary source (1). At three or more the row is marked consequential, and the Weight filter narrows to those.

What is refused. The word in its unrelated senses, opinion and entertainment that uses the vocabulary without being about anyone's treatment, sponsored content, and market copy. The status line reports how many stories each harvest refused.

Translation. The Translate control at the top renders the headlines and snippets on screen into any of 26 languages, through a free public translation endpoint — no account, no key, nothing stored. A translated row is marked as machine translation and keeps the publisher's original wording on hover, because a machine rendering is not the publisher's words. If the endpoint is unavailable the row simply stays in its original language rather than emptying. Translation applies to what is on screen; scroll further or widen a filter and press the control again.

Region. Placement runs three levels deep. Pick a region and a second row opens with its subregions; pick one of those and a third row lists the places inside it — Latin America to the Amazon Basin to the Brazilian Amazon, Oceania to Australia to the Northern Territory. Each row counts what it holds, so an empty subregion is visible rather than hidden, and every story files under the subregion and region above whatever place it names.

Limits worth knowing. The gate is mechanical: it reads words, not meaning. Standing is assigned per wire rather than per article. Google News caps a query at roughly 100 results over about 30 days. Coverage is uneven by language and the counts show it rather than hiding it.

Every wire read on the last harvest, with its standing: what it returned, and how many of its stories were refused. A wire that could not be reached says so rather than disappearing.
